### SECTION 3 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Supreme Court of Pakistan heard Civil Petitions No. 3451 to 3464 of 2017 and Civil Petitions No. 3473 to 3651 of 2017 filed by WAPDA seeking leave to appeal against the Federal Service Tribunal's (FST) order dated 17.07.2017. The core dispute centers on WAPDA's persistent refusal to grant "generation allowance" and attendant facilities (revised free electricity units, overtime allowance) to general/non-generation employees of Tarbela Power Plant from their initial appointment dates, despite final judgments of the FST and Supreme Court dating back to 1997 and 2009. WAPDA attempted to defeat full compliance by issuing an implementation order effective only prospectively from 31.03.2015. 

On 17.07.2017, the FST allowed the employees' Miscellaneous Petitions, ordering complete implementation from initial appointment dates within one month under threat of coercive action. The Supreme Court deprecated WAPDA's conduct, holding that re-agitating settled issues reeked of *mala fide*, constituted a gross abuse of process, and violated the constitutional mandate binding all authorities to act in aid of the Supreme Court. Refusing leave to appeal, the Supreme Court dismissed all petitions, ordered immediate implementation within 07 days, and imposed a personal special cost of Rs. 50,000/- on the Chairman, WAPDA, payable out of his own pocket to the Fatimid Foundation.

### SECTION 4 — DETAILED FACTUAL BACKGROUND

#### 1. Initial Grant and First Litigation Round (1997)
- "Generation allowance" was originally granted exclusively to the generation staff of Tarbela Power Plant, while non-generation staff were excluded.
- Aggrieved non-generation employees filed departmental representations before the competent authority, which were dismissed.
- The aggrieved employees appealed to the Federal Service Tribunal (FST), Islamabad. On **22.03.1997**, the FST allowed their appeals and granted the allowance.
- WAPDA challenged the FST order before the Supreme Court via Civil Petitions No. 840 to 844 of 1997. On **10.10.1997**, the Supreme Court dismissed WAPDA's petitions, affirming the FST's judgment.

#### 2. Second Litigation Round for Similarly Placed Employees (2009–2010)
- A second group of employees ("255 in number") claimed identical benefits as "similarly placed employees."
- Their departmental representations were rejected by competent authorities.
- On appeal, the FST allowed their claims on **22.10.2009**.
- WAPDA challenged this decision in the Supreme Court via Civil Petitions No. 2281 to 2534 of 2009. The Supreme Court dismissed WAPDA's petitions on **12.10.2010**.

#### 3. Non-Implementation and First Round of Execution Petitions (2015–2016)
- WAPDA failed to implement the final judgments. The employees filed Miscellaneous Petitions (MPs) before the FST seeking implementation.
- On **31.03.2015**, the FST allowed the MPs, directing WAPDA to implement the decisions.
- WAPDA appealed to the Supreme Court; the Supreme Court dismissed WAPDA's appeals on **05.10.2015**.
- WAPDA filed Review Petitions before the Supreme Court, which were dismissed on **24.02.2016**.

#### 4. Defective Implementation and FST Order (2015–2017)
- Following the dismissal of its legal remedies, WAPDA issued an order giving effect to the judgments *only* from **31.03.2015** onwards, rather than from the date of the employees' initial appointments.
- Employees filed fresh Miscellaneous Petitions (MPs No. 2630 to 2715 of 2016, etc.) before the FST seeking complete implementation in true perspective from their initial appointment dates, including generation allowance, revised free electricity units, and overtime allowance.
- On **17.07.2017**, the FST allowed the MPs, holding that WAPDA was bound to implement the orders in true perspective. The FST granted WAPDA "one month more time to implement the judgments/order in letter and spirit", failing which coercive measures would be taken under law.

#### 5. Appeal to Supreme Court (15.12.2017)
- WAPDA filed Civil Petitions No. 3451 to 3464 of 2017 and Civil Petitions No. 3473 to 3651 of 2017 challenging the FST's implementation order dated 17.07.2017. The Supreme Court consolidated all petitions for disposal via a single order on **15.12.2017**.

### SECTION 5 — LEGAL ISSUES FRAMED
1. Whether non-generation / general employees of Tarbela Power Plant are legally entitled to the generation allowance and other facilities (revised free electricity units, overtime allowance) from the date of their initial appointment on par with generation staff.
2. Whether WAPDA functionaries could lawfully restrict the execution of final judgments of the FST and Supreme Court to effective date 31.03.2015 instead of implementing them from initial appointment dates.
3. Whether the FST exceeded its jurisdiction or interfered with the executive's policy-making domain by directing the grant of generation allowance to staff not directly performing generation duties.
4. Whether the repeated filing of petitions and re-litigation of finalized issues by State functionaries constitutes *mala fide* conduct and a gross abuse of the process of the Court.
5. Whether the refusal of executive authorities to comply with judicial mandates violates the constitutional directive requiring all executive and judicial authorities throughout Pakistan to act in aid of the Supreme Court.

### SECTION 8 — COURT'S ANALYSIS & FINDINGS

1. **On Merits and Executive Policy (Issues 1 & 3):**
   The points raised by WAPDA regarding the scope, policy, and applicability of the generation allowance have been repeatedly considered, addressed, and explicitly rejected by both the Service Tribunal and the Supreme Court in multiple previous rounds of litigation. The issue reached finality years ago and cannot be re-agitated.

2. **On Abuse of Judicial Process and Mala Fide Conduct (Issues 2 & 4):**
   WAPDA’s attempt to reopen settled points "for the umpteenth time" reeks of *mala fide*, constitutes a "denial of the fruits of justice", and represents a "gross abuse of the process of this Court." The Court expressed being "disappointed, shocked and perturbed by the conduct of the State functionaries", holding that their blatant violation and conscious refusal to implement decisions must be deprecated in the strongest terms.

3. **On Constitutional Obligation (Issue 5):**
   Under the command of the Constitution of Pakistan, all executive and judicial authorities throughout Pakistan are bound to act in aid of the Supreme Court. The refusal of State functionaries to abide by judicial orders threatens "to strike the very foundation of constitutionalism, rule of law and administration of justice." While minded to take more stringent action, the Court opted to exercise judicial restraint.

### SECTION 14 — GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS
- **Generation Allowance:** A specialized financial benefit granted to power plant employees working at power stations (such as Tarbela Power Plant).
- **In Letter and Spirit:** Strict, complete, and genuine compliance following both the explicit text and the underlying purpose of a legal order.
- **Mala Fide:** Bad faith; intent to deceive, evade, or abuse legal processes.
- **Act in Aid of this Court:** The constitutional mandate requiring all state, executive, and judicial bodies in Pakistan to enforce and support the execution of Supreme Court orders.
- **Judicial Restraint:** A principle where a court refrains from exercising full punitive power, selecting a milder remedy despite severe non-compliance.
