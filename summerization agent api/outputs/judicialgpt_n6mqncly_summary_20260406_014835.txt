### SECTION 3 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY  
The petitioners, Wapda through its Chairman and others, are seeking leave to appeal against an order dated "17.07.2017" passed by the Federal Service Tribunal, Islamabad. The order allowed various Miscellaneous Petitions filed by the respondents, seeking implementation of judgments/orders of the Service Tribunal. The petitioners have been directed to implement these judgments/orders within "one month", with the possibility of "coercive measures" being taken against them if they fail to comply. The matter involves the grant of "generation allowance" to certain employees of Tarbela Power Plant, which was allowed by the Service Tribunal but contested by Wapda. The Supreme Court is to decide on the legality and tenability of the Service Tribunal's order and the petitioners' challenges to it.

---

### SECTION 4 — DETAILED FACTUAL BACKGROUND
The petitioners are seeking leave to appeal against an order dated "17.07.2017" passed by the Federal Service Tribunal, Islamabad, in Miscellaneous Petitions No.2630 to 2715 of 2016. The Civil Petitions No.3451 to 3464 of 2017 and Civil Petitions No.3473 to 3651 of 2017 are being decided through a single order as they raise common questions of law and facts. The date of hearing is "15.12.2017".

The issue pertains to "generation allowance" which was allowed to generation staff of Tarbela Power Plant. Other employees of the Plant, having been denied such allowance, filed representations before the competent authority for grant of such allowance, which were dismissed. They then approached the Service Tribunal by way of appeals, which were allowed on "22.03.1997". The petitioners challenged the order of the Service Tribunal before the Supreme Court through Civil Petitions No.840 to 844 of 1997, which were dismissed, vide order dated "10.10.1997".

Subsequently, some other employees, 255 in number, also claimed the same benefits granted to other similarly placed employees but were denied by the competent authorities. Their matters also ended up before the Service Tribunal, which allowed their appeals on "22.10.2009".

The petitioners had "exhausted all remedies before all fora" and were seeking implementation of a judgment with effect from "31.03.2015". This was in violation of orders dated "22.03.1997" and "22.10.2009" passed by the Service Tribunal and upheld by the Court.

The employees had to approach the Service Tribunal again for implementation of judgments. An impugned order was passed on "17.07.2017", allowing certain relief to the petitioners.

The State functionaries have "blatantly violated and consciously refused to implement the judgments of the Service Tribunal as well as this Court with impunity". The court expresses disappointment, shock, and perturbation at the conduct of the State functionaries.

The petitioners are to "forthwith implement the orders" and submit a report within "07 days of the date of receipt of this order".

### SECTION 5 — LEGAL ISSUES FRAMED
1. Whether the Service Tribunal has erred in law by interfering with the policy-making domain of the Executive.
2. Whether the petitioners' challenges to the judgments of the Service Tribunal are legally and factually tenable.
3. Whether the grant of generation allowance by Wapda is in accordance with the underlying philosophy, object, and purpose behind the policy.
4. Whether the petitioners' failure to implement the judgments of the Service Tribunal and the Court constitutes a gross abuse of the process of the Court.
5. Whether the respondents are entitled to the relief sought, including payment of generation allowance and extension of facilities like revised free electricity units and overtime allowance.

### SECTION 8 — COURT'S ANALYSIS & FINDINGS
The court found that the petitioners' points have been repeatedly considered and rejected, and their attempt to reopen these issues "reeks of mala fide" and constitutes a "gross abuse of the process of this Court". The court deprecates the conduct of State functionaries in the strongest terms.

The court also found that the Service Tribunal has erred in law by interfering with the policy-making domain of the Executive.

---

### SECTION 9 — DECISION / ORDER / OPERATIVE CLAUSE
The court decided to "exercise judicial restraint" instead of taking more stringent action. It also decided to dismiss the petitions and refuse leave to appeal.

A special cost of Rs.50,000/- was imposed on the Chairman, Wapda, in his personal capacity, to be paid out of his own pocket and deposited with the Fatimid Foundation.

The petitioners were directed to "forthwith implement the orders" and submit a report within "07 days of the date of receipt of this order".
